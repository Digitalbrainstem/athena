# 03 — Travel Mechanics

> Travel in Nexus Academy is a progression system unto itself. Players start walking
> around a village at age 2 and end up navigating between star systems at age 22.
> Every expansion in travel range requires genuinely deeper knowledge — building
> vehicles teaches engineering, fuel requires chemistry, navigation requires math,
> and the journey to the stars requires mastering physics from Newtonian mechanics
> through general relativity. The journey literally expands with what you know.

---

## The Travel Progression

```
Walking the garden
  → Riding to the next biome
    → Sailing the ocean
      → Flying across continents
        → Orbiting the planet
          → Landing on the moon
            → Hohmann transfers to other planets
              → Gravity assists to the outer system
                → First wormhole to a nearby star
                  → Across the galaxy to the Nexus Core
```

Each stage requires MORE knowledge to unlock. You can't buy a bigger ship. You can't
grind XP to skip ahead. You must understand propulsion to build an engine. You must
understand chemistry to synthesize fuel. You must understand orbital mechanics to plot
a course. Knowledge is the key to the universe — literally.

## Stage 1 — Local (Foundation, typically ages 2–5)

**Range:** The village, the garden, the companion's home, and a few nearby landmarks.

**How it works:**
- Player walks (tap to move, or voice: "Let's go to the garden!")
- Companion leads the way — the player can't get lost
- Paths are simple and clear — no mazes, no confusing intersections
- World is small, cozy, safe — like a neighborhood

**What it teaches:**
- Spatial reasoning: left/right, near/far, above/below
- Direction: "Turn this way!" (quarter turn, half turn — building radian intuition)
- Distance: "The tree is far away. The flower is close."
- Sequence: "First the bridge, then the hill, then the lake."
- Counting steps: "How many steps to the big tree?"

**Example interaction:**
```
COMPANION: "I want to visit the waterfall! It's past the big tree 
and across the bridge. Can you lead the way?"

[Player taps/walks toward the tree]

COMPANION: "That's the big tree! Now which way is the bridge?
I think it's to the LEFT!"

[Player turns left]

COMPANION: "Yes! There's the bridge! Let's count the steps across.
One... two... three... four... five! Five steps!"
```

## Stage 2 — Regional (Discovery, typically ages 6–10)

**Range:** Multiple biomes connected by paths, roads, rivers, and coastline.

**How it works:**
- Map unlocks with a compass rose — player can see biome locations
- Walking is primary, but animals (horses, large birds) are ridable for faster travel
- Simple boats for river and coastal travel
- First crafted vehicle: a basic cart (wheels, axle, platform)

**New travel mechanics:**
- **Compass navigation:** Cardinal directions (N/S/E/W), then intercardinal (NE, SW…)
- **Map coordinates:** Grid system introduced. "The Workshop is at grid C-4."
- **Distance/speed/time:** "The Trading Post is 6 km away. Walking at 3 km/h, how long?"
- **Terrain effects:** Hills are slower. Rivers require bridges or boats. Deserts need water.
- **Basic vehicle building:** Cart needs wheels (circles!), axle (cylinder), platform (rectangle). Load capacity introduces weight vs strength.

**What it teaches:**
- Coordinate systems (grid-based, then Cartesian)
- Speed, distance, time relationships (d = s × t)
- Basic engineering (simple machines — wheel, lever, pulley)
- Geography (terrain types, water features, climate zones)
- Resource planning (pack enough food and water for the journey)

**Example interaction:**
```
COMPANION: "The Alchemist's Lab is at coordinates (7, 3) on the map.
We're at (2, 1). That's 5 squares east and 2 squares north.
Which way should we go first?"

[Player navigates on the map]

COMPANION: "We could go straight, but there's a mountain range at 
row 2! We'd have to go AROUND it. Let's find the shortest path 
that avoids the mountains..."
```

## Stage 3 — Continental (Builder, typically ages 11–14)

**Range:** Entire continents, oceans, and diverse climate zones.

**How it works:**
- Build ships (sailing vessels, then steam-powered)
- Build hot air balloons and gliders
- Navigate by stars and compass bearing
- Manage fuel, food, water, and crew supplies
- Weather affects travel: wind direction matters for sailing, storms are dangerous

**New travel mechanics:**

**Ship building:**
- Hull design: buoyancy calculations (Archimedes' principle)
- Sail configuration: how wind angle translates to forward thrust
- Steam power: boiler design, fuel consumption rates, heat transfer
- Load calculation: cargo weight vs. hull displacement

**Navigation:**
- Bearing and heading (degrees, then radians)
- Triangulation using landmarks or celestial objects
- Dead reckoning: speed × time × heading = estimated position
- Introduction to latitude/longitude
- Current and wind vector addition: "Wind from NW at 20 km/h, current from S at 5 km/h — what's your actual course?"

**Fuel chemistry:**
- Wood → coal → oil as fuel sources
- Energy density comparisons (kJ/kg)
- Combustion chemistry: CₓHₓ + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O + energy
- Efficiency: not all chemical energy becomes motion

**What it teaches:**
- Trigonometry (navigation, bearing, wind vectors)
- Chemistry (combustion, energy density, fuel types)
- Physics (buoyancy, fluid dynamics, aerodynamics)
- Engineering (structural design, propulsion systems)
- Geography (ocean currents, climate, global wind patterns)
- Resource management (fuel, food, water budgets for long voyages)

**Example: Building a sailing ship**
```
COMPANION: "The Shipyard has oak planks, canvas, and rope.
Let's design our ship!

First — the hull. It needs to displace enough water to support
the cargo. Our total weight is 2000 kg. Water density is 
1000 kg/m³. So we need at least 2 m³ of displacement.

How wide and deep should the hull be if it's 8 meters long?"

[Player calculates: width × depth × 8 = 2, so width × depth = 0.25 m²]
[Player could choose 0.5m × 0.5m, or 1m × 0.25m, etc.]

COMPANION: "That's the MINIMUM. In practice, we want at least 
50% extra for safety. Waves, wind, and that heavy anchor..."
```

## Stage 4 — Planetary (Innovator, typically ages 15–18)

**Range:** Entire planet surface, atmosphere, low orbit.

**How it works:**
- Build aircraft (propeller planes → jets)
- Build submarines for ocean exploration
- Build the first rocket to reach orbit
- Circumnavigation quests: great circle routes
- Atmospheric science: air pressure, weather patterns at altitude

**New travel mechanics:**

**Aircraft:**
- Bernoulli's principle: how wings generate lift
- Lift/drag coefficients: wing shape matters
- Thrust-to-weight ratio: how much engine power per kilogram
- Fuel consumption vs. altitude vs. speed trade-offs
- Navigation: great circle routes, latitude/longitude, heading correction for wind

**First rocket:**
- Tsiolkovsky rocket equation: Δv = Isp × g₀ × ln(m₀/mf)
- Staging: why multi-stage rockets are more efficient
- Escape velocity: √(2GM/r) — how fast to leave the surface
- Fuel mass ratio: most of your rocket is fuel. The tyranny of the rocket equation.
- Trajectory: launch angle, gravity turn, orbital insertion

**What it teaches:**
- Aerodynamics (lift, drag, Bernoulli's principle)
- Orbital mechanics fundamentals (escape velocity, orbital velocity)
- Calculus applications (fuel consumption rates, optimization)
- Atmospheric physics (pressure, temperature, density vs. altitude)
- Advanced chemistry (high-energy fuels, specific impulse)

**Example: Building the first rocket**
```
COMPANION: "We've built planes. We've sailed the oceans. We've explored
every corner of the planet. But what's UP THERE?"

[Player looks at the sky — stars, moons, a faint planet visible]

COMPANION: "To reach orbit, we need to go FAST. Orbital velocity at 
200 km altitude is about 7.8 km/s. That's 28,000 km/h.

Our best jet engine produces 2 km/s. We need a fundamentally 
different approach. What if instead of pushing against air, 
we pushed against the exhaust itself?"

[Leads into rocket propulsion, Newton's third law, and the 
Tsiolkovsky equation over several quest steps]
```

## Stage 5 — Interplanetary (Innovator–Creator, typically ages 17+)

**Range:** The solar system — moons, planets, asteroid belt, gas giants.

**How it works:**
- Hohmann transfer orbits (minimum-energy trajectories between planets)
- Gravity assists (slingshot maneuvers around planets)
- Δv budgets: planning how much velocity change each maneuver needs
- Multi-body orbital mechanics: patched conic approximations
- In-space propulsion: chemical → ion → nuclear thermal

**New travel mechanics:**

**Orbital mechanics:**
- Hohmann transfers: the fuel-efficient elliptical path between orbits
- Transfer windows: planets must be in the right alignment
- Gravity assists: free Δv from planetary flybys — requires precise timing and geometry
- Orbital rendezvous: matching orbits with a station or another ship
- Aerobraking: using atmospheric drag to slow down at destination

**Propulsion evolution:**
| Propulsion Type | Specific Impulse | Thrust | Knowledge Required |
|----------------|-----------------|--------|-------------------|
| Chemical (LOX/LH2) | 450s | High | Chemistry, thermodynamics |
| Ion drive | 3,000s | Very low | Electromagnetism, plasma physics |
| Nuclear thermal | 900s | Medium | Nuclear physics, radiation |
| Solar sail | ∞ (no fuel) | Tiny | Radiation pressure, orbital mechanics |

**Navigation in space:**
- No "north" in space — reference frames matter
- Prograde/retrograde, normal/anti-normal, radial in/out
- Patched conics: different gravitational domains
- Communication delays: light-speed lag between planets
- Time dilation preview: at high velocities, clocks disagree (special relativity intro)

**What it teaches:**
- Orbital mechanics (Kepler's laws, vis-viva equation, transfer orbits)
- Advanced physics (gravity, electromagnetism, nuclear reactions)
- Advanced chemistry (propellant chemistry, specific impulse, combustion efficiency)
- Engineering (life support, thermal management, structural design for space)
- Mathematics (conic sections, differential equations, optimization)

**Example: First interplanetary mission**
```
COMPANION: "The second planet has resources we need. It's currently 
at 45° ahead of us in its orbit. Our orbital radius is 150 million km,
theirs is 108 million km.

A Hohmann transfer would take about 146 days. The launch window 
opens in 22 game-days.

Let's calculate the Δv budget:
- Trans-planetary injection: ~3.5 km/s
- Arrival orbit insertion: ~2.7 km/s  
- Total: 6.2 km/s

Our current engine has 4 km/s of Δv. Options?"

[Player must either: upgrade the engine, reduce ship mass, add a 
gravity assist past the inner moon, or build a refueling station]
```

## Stage 6 — Interstellar (Creator, ages 18+)

**Range:** Nearby star systems, then across the galaxy to the Nexus Core.

**How it works:**
- FTL is not magic — it requires understanding the physics of spacetime
- Wormholes: stable traversable wormholes require exotic matter (negative energy)
- Alcubierre-like drives: contract space ahead, expand behind — requires deep GR understanding
- The journey to distant stars takes REAL mastery of physics and mathematics
- Each new propulsion breakthrough requires genuinely harder science

**New travel mechanics:**

**Wormhole navigation:**
- General relativity: spacetime curvature, geodesics
- Exotic matter: negative energy density (theoretical but grounded in real physics)
- Wormhole stability: maintaining the throat against collapse
- Entrance/exit mapping: where in spacetime does each end connect?
- Tidal forces: how to survive the passage (spaghettification avoidance)

**Alcubierre-type drive:**
- Metric tensor manipulation (graduate-level GR)
- Energy requirements: how much exotic matter per lightyear
- Causal structure: causality paradoxes and resolution
- Navigation: course plotting through warped spacetime

**Fuel chemistry at this stage:**
| Fuel Type | Energy per kg | Required Knowledge |
|-----------|--------------|-------------------|
| Fusion (D-T) | 337 TJ/kg | Nuclear physics, plasma confinement |
| Antimatter | 90 PJ/kg | Particle physics, containment engineering |
| Zero-point (theoretical) | Varies | Quantum field theory |

**Stellar cartography:**
- Parsecs and light-years as real units
- Stellar classification (O, B, A, F, G, K, M)
- Binary/trinary star systems: complex orbital dynamics
- Interstellar medium: dust, radiation, magnetic fields
- Reference frames: coordinate systems that work at galactic scale

**What it teaches:**
- General relativity (spacetime curvature, metrics, geodesics)
- Quantum field theory (basics — zero-point energy, vacuum fluctuations)
- Advanced mathematics (tensor calculus, differential geometry)
- Nuclear and particle physics (fusion, antimatter production/containment)
- Philosophy of physics (causality, reference frames, the nature of spacetime)

**The Nexus Core:**

The Nexus Core sits at the center of the galaxy. Reaching it is the ultimate travel
challenge — it requires mastery of every propulsion system, every navigation technique,
every scientific discipline. The journey IS the final exam that isn't a test.

See [10-STORY_NARRATIVE.md](10-STORY_NARRATIVE.md) for what the player finds there.

## Vehicle Building System

Vehicles are built in the Shipyard biome using the crafting system (see [05-GAMEPLAY_LOOPS.md](05-GAMEPLAY_LOOPS.md)). Each vehicle has real physical properties.

### Vehicle Properties

```
Vehicle
├── Hull/Frame
│   ├── Material (wood, metal, composite, exotic alloy)
│   ├── Mass (kg)
│   ├── Structural integrity (load rating)
│   └── Drag coefficient
├── Propulsion
│   ├── Type (sail, steam, jet, rocket, ion, fusion, wormhole)
│   ├── Thrust (N)
│   ├── Specific impulse (s)
│   └── Fuel consumption rate (kg/s)
├── Fuel
│   ├── Type (wood, coal, kerosene, LOX/LH2, deuterium, antimatter)
│   ├── Capacity (kg)
│   └── Energy density (J/kg)
├── Navigation
│   ├── Compass (Stage 2+)
│   ├── Sextant/star chart (Stage 3+)
│   ├── Radar/lidar (Stage 4+)
│   └── Relativistic nav computer (Stage 6)
├── Life Support (space vehicles)
│   ├── Oxygen supply
│   ├── Thermal control
│   └── Radiation shielding
└── Cargo/Passenger Capacity
    ├── Max payload (kg)
    └── Crew positions
```

### Building Process

1. **Design:** Choose components from available technology
2. **Calculate:** The game requires calculations for key specs (e.g., displacement for boats, Δv for rockets)
3. **Build:** Assemble components in the Shipyard
4. **Test:** Launch/deploy — physics engine validates the design
5. **Iterate:** If it fails (boat sinks, bridge breaks, rocket doesn't reach orbit), diagnose and redesign

Failure is always safe and educational. A sinking boat doesn't kill the player — the companion
rescues them and says "I think the hull was too small for the cargo. Let's check the buoyancy
calculation." The failure IS the lesson.

## Navigation Tools (By Stage)

| Stage | Navigation Tools | Accuracy |
|-------|-----------------|----------|
| 1 — Local | Companion guidance, landmarks | Always accurate — can't get lost |
| 2 — Regional | Map grid, compass, landmarks | Grid-square accuracy |
| 3 — Continental | Sextant, star charts, bearing | Degree-level accuracy, can get lost |
| 4 — Planetary | Inertial navigation, GPS concept | Meter-level accuracy |
| 5 — Interplanetary | Orbital computer, patched conics | Must calculate transfer orbits |
| 6 — Interstellar | Relativistic nav, spacetime mapping | Tensor-level coordinate transforms |

At each stage, the player can use the simpler tools from previous stages too. An Innovator-tier player
navigating by stars on the ocean is using the same trigonometry as a Creator-tier player plotting a
gravity assist — just at different scale.

## Travel and Learning Integration

The travel system isn't a separate mechanic — it's deeply integrated with the subject
mapping (see [07-SUBJECT_MAPPING.md](07-SUBJECT_MAPPING.md)):

| Travel Challenge | Subjects Required |
|-----------------|------------------|
| "Build a cart to carry ore from the mine" | Engineering (wheels), Math (load capacity) |
| "Sail to the island using the wind" | Physics (vectors), Trig (angles), Geography (currents) |
| "Fly over the mountain range" | Physics (lift), Chemistry (fuel), Math (altitude vs. air density) |
| "Reach orbit and dock with the station" | Physics (orbital mechanics), Calculus (Δv), Engineering (life support) |
| "Navigate to the third planet" | Orbital mechanics (Hohmann), Chemistry (fuel), Math (transfer windows) |
| "Build a wormhole to a distant star" | General relativity, Quantum field theory, Advanced math |

Every trip is a learning opportunity. Even "walk to the next biome" teaches spatial
reasoning at the Foundation tier. The game never wastes travel time — the journey
itself is education.
