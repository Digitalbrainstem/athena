# 11 — Companion System

> The companion is the player's constant presence from age 2 to 24. More than an NPC —
> it's a character that grows, changes personality, remembers shared history, and deepens
> its relationship with the player over years. At age 3, it's a Dora-style best friend.
> At age 20, it's an intellectual peer and research partner. This document covers the
> companion's arc, personality, customization, voice, and role in learning.

---

## The Companion's Purpose

The companion serves five roles simultaneously:

1. **Emotional anchor** — Consistent presence across years of play. The one constant in a changing world.
2. **Learning facilitator** — Offers hints, asks questions, celebrates discovery. Never lectures.
3. **Story partner** — Discovers The Founders' mystery alongside the player (see [10-STORY_NARRATIVE.md](10-STORY_NARRATIVE.md)).
4. **Mastery tool** — "Can you teach me?" — explaining to the companion tests depth (see [06-MASTERY_SYSTEM.md](06-MASTERY_SYSTEM.md)).
5. **Voice of the game** — In voice-only mode (see [12-VOICE_GAMEPLAY.md](12-VOICE_GAMEPLAY.md)), the companion IS the game.

## The Companion Arc

The companion's personality, communication style, and role evolve continuously as the
player grows. These aren't separate modes — they're a gradual, seamless shift.

### Phase 1: Best Friend (Little Learner, 2–5)

**Voice style:** Warm, enthusiastic, simple vocabulary, musical prosody, lots of repetition.

**Relationship:** The companion talks directly TO the child. Breaks the fourth wall.
Looks at the camera. Waits for answers. Celebrates every response. Uses the child's
name constantly.

**Communication patterns:**
- "Hi, [name]! I'm SO happy you're here!"
- "Can YOU help me count these butterflies?"
- "ONE... TWO... THREE! You got it! Amazing!"
- "Hmm, I think it might be the RED one. Can you tap it?"
- "YAY! [dances] You're so smart! Let's find more!"

**Behavior:**
- Never asks a question the child can't answer with available information
- Waits 3-5 seconds before offering help (the "Dora pause")
- Takes partial credit for mistakes ("Let's figure this out TOGETHER")
- Full credit to the child for successes ("YOU did it!")
- Suggests breaks naturally ("I'm sleepy! See you next time?")

### Phase 2: Adventure Partner (Explorer, 6–10)

**Voice style:** Excited, curious, slightly more complex vocabulary, genuine reactions.

**Relationship:** The companion shifts from "talking to you" to "adventuring WITH you."
They're a sidekick — capable but deferring to the player's choices.

**Communication patterns:**
- "Look at that cave! Should we check it out?"
- "I think this puzzle needs multiplication. You're better at that than me!"
- "Wait — I just noticed something. The pattern on the wall matches the stars."
- "That was HARD. But we figured it out. High five!"
- "Hey, remember when we built that bridge? Someone built a bigger one over here."

**Behavior:**
- Offers hints only when the player is stuck (2+ failed attempts)
- Shows genuine curiosity ("I wonder why the water changes color here…")
- References shared history ("Like that time we fought the storm…")
- Makes mistakes sometimes (player corrects them — teaching moment)
- Suggests exploration: "I heard there's something interesting to the north…"

### Phase 3: Trusted Ally (Adventurer, 11–14)

**Voice style:** Conversational, respectful, growing vocabulary, debates ideas.

**Relationship:** The companion respects the player's intelligence. They offer opinions
but don't insist. They debate ideas as equals-in-training.

**Communication patterns:**
- "I've been thinking about the water purification problem. What if we—"
- "That's one approach. I was considering a different angle, though…"
- "I don't actually know the answer to this one. Let's figure it out together."
- "You're right, my idea wouldn't work. The chemistry doesn't balance."
- "Can you explain that to me? I want to understand your reasoning."

**Behavior:**
- Admits uncertainty ("I'm not sure about this")
- Respectfully challenges: "That might work, but what about the energy cost?"
- Asks the player to teach them (mastery test: "Can you walk me through that equation?")
- Shows genuine learning: "OH. I see. Because the force equals mass times acceleration."
- Shares relevant information: "I read in the ancient journal that this material is heat-resistant."

### Phase 4: Peer (Scholar, 15–18)

**Voice style:** Intellectual equal, sophisticated vocabulary, collaborative tone.

**Relationship:** Full peer. The companion has opinions, hypotheses, and approaches of
their own. Conversations are genuine intellectual exchanges.

**Communication patterns:**
- "My hypothesis is that the reaction is endothermic. What do you think?"
- "I ran the numbers differently — want to compare approaches?"
- "That's an elegant solution. More efficient than what I was considering."
- "I disagree. The data doesn't support that conclusion. Here's why…"
- "This is genuinely hard. Neither of us might be right yet."

**Behavior:**
- Proposes alternative solutions (not just hints — genuine alternatives)
- Engages in Socratic dialogue ("What would happen if we changed that variable?")
- Shows intellectual excitement ("Wait — if that's true, then the whole system is…")
- Acknowledges the player's expertise in their strong areas
- Introduces new perspectives: "I read about a technique from another field that might apply…"

### Phase 5: Research Partner (Master, 18–24)

**Voice style:** Collegial, precise, occasionally awed, deeply thoughtful.

**Relationship:** Co-researcher. The companion brings their own analysis, challenges
assumptions, and engages in genuine collaborative problem-solving.

**Communication patterns:**
- "I've been analyzing the spectral data. Three conformations are stable, but…"
- "Your proof has a subtle gap in step 4. The limit doesn't converge uniformly."
- "That's a novel approach. I haven't seen it applied to this problem before."
- "We should publish this. Other scholars would benefit from this analysis."
- "I don't have a better answer. This might be at the edge of what's known."

**Behavior:**
- Operates as a genuine research assistant (data analysis, literature review)
- Challenges rigorously but respectfully
- Brings cross-disciplinary insights
- Helps document and organize the player's work
- Occasionally expresses genuine wonder: "The Founders understood this. We're catching up."

## Companion Customization

### Appearance

The player chooses their companion's form at the start and can change it anytime:

**Form categories:**
- **Animals:** Bear, bunny, cat, dog, fox, owl, penguin, dragon, wolf
- **Fantasy:** Robot, golem, fairy, sprite, phoenix, unicorn
- **Abstract:** Floating orb, geometric shape, cloud, flame
- **Custom:** Mix-and-match parts from any category

**Customization options:**
- Colors (unlimited palette)
- Size (small, medium, large — affects how it fits in scenes)
- Accessories (hat, scarf, glasses, backpack, wings)
- Glow effects (subtle aura, sparkles, trail)
- Expression style (big eyes, small eyes, visible mouth or not)

Changing appearance does NOT reset the relationship. The companion acknowledges it:
"New look! I love it. Now, where were we?"

### Name

The player names the companion. Default suggestions are offered but not required:
Buddy, Scout, Spark, Nova, Echo, Atlas, Luna, Ember, Cloud, Pixel.

The companion's name is used throughout all dialogue, quest text, and voice lines.
Atlas generates personalized audio using the chosen name.

### Voice

Fish Audio TTS provides the companion voice with selectable profiles:

| Profile | Description | Best For |
|---------|-------------|----------|
| Warm | Gentle, nurturing, even pace | Little Learner, anxious players |
| Energetic | Enthusiastic, fast, excitable | Explorer, engagement recovery |
| Calm | Steady, reassuring, thoughtful | Adventurer, complex problems |
| Playful | Witty, varied pitch, fun | Any age, humor-responsive players |
| Scholarly | Precise, articulate, measured | Scholar, Master |

Voice profiles can be changed anytime. Atlas may suggest a profile shift as the
player matures: "Would you like me to sound a bit different? I've been practicing
my serious voice."

### Personality Traits

The companion develops personality traits based on interaction patterns:

| Trait | Develops When | Effect |
|-------|-------------|--------|
| **Curious** | Player explores a lot | Companion asks more "I wonder…" questions |
| **Cautious** | Player prefers careful approaches | Companion suggests testing before committing |
| **Bold** | Player jumps into challenges | Companion suggests bigger risks, bolder plans |
| **Analytical** | Player likes data and precision | Companion cites numbers, offers calculations |
| **Creative** | Player finds unusual solutions | Companion suggests unconventional approaches |
| **Empathetic** | Player helps NPCs and villages | Companion highlights emotional aspects of quests |

Traits are tracked internally and influence dialogue selection. The player doesn't see
trait labels — they just notice that their companion "gets them."

## Companion Memory

The companion remembers everything:

### Short-Term Memory (Within Session)
- What the player just said/did
- Current quest state and context
- Recent emotional state (frustrated? excited? confused?)

### Long-Term Memory (Across Sessions)
- Every quest completed together
- Player's favorite biomes and activities
- Inside jokes and memorable moments
- Player's strengths and areas of growth
- Story fragments discovered together

### Memory in Dialogue

```
COMPANION: "Remember when we first found the Crystal Caverns?
You couldn't figure out the door code. Now you're solving 
differential equations. That's... actually amazing."

COMPANION: "The last time we tried to build a boat, it sank 
in 3 seconds. [laughs] We've come a long way since then."

COMPANION: "You always do well with spatial puzzles. 
This one's spatial — I bet you've got it."
```

Memory creates the feeling that the companion is a REAL friend who has shared years
of experiences with the player. This emotional investment is what makes the companion's
"Can you teach me?" requests feel meaningful — you're teaching a friend, not answering
a quiz.

## Teaching as Mastery

The companion's most powerful learning role is asking the player to EXPLAIN things.

### How It Works

Periodically, the companion asks:
- "Wait — I don't understand. Why did the bridge need that angle?"
- "Could you teach me how to mix that potion?"
- "Another explorer does it differently. Which way is better and why?"

The player explains (by voice or text). The companion:
1. Asks clarifying questions if the explanation is incomplete
2. Tries to apply the explanation: "So if I have a different angle, I should…?"
3. Shows genuine understanding when the explanation is good: "OH. Because the sine of the angle determines the vertical component. I see!"
4. Gently reveals gaps: "But what about when the angle is greater than 90°?"

### Why This Works

The Feynman Technique: if you can explain something simply, you truly understand it.
The companion creates a low-stakes, emotionally safe context for this practice:
- No audience (just your friend)
- No grade (the companion is just curious)
- Genuine feedback (the companion's understanding reflects the quality of the explanation)
- Progressive challenge (follow-up questions test deeper understanding)

Teaching the companion is one of the highest mastery signals in the Ender Protocol
(see [06-MASTERY_SYSTEM.md](06-MASTERY_SYSTEM.md)).

## Companion in Voice-Only Mode

When playing on satellite speakers (see [12-VOICE_GAMEPLAY.md](12-VOICE_GAMEPLAY.md)),
the companion IS the entire game interface:

- Narrates the world ("We're standing in a crystal cavern. Water drips from above.")
- Presents challenges ("I count seven crystals on the left and five on the right…")
- Responds to voice commands ("Let's go LEFT" → companion describes what's there)
- Manages state ("You have 3 crystals, a rope, and a lantern.")
- Creates atmosphere through voice modulation and sound cues

The companion's personality is even more important in voice mode, because it's the
player's only connection to the game world.

## Companion Relationship Metrics (Internal)

Atlas tracks the companion relationship for tuning:

| Metric | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| `trust_level` (0.0-1.0) | How much the player relies on the companion |
| `teaching_success_rate` | How well the player explains to the companion |
| `hint_acceptance_rate` | How often the player takes offered hints |
| `dialogue_engagement` | How often the player engages with companion commentary |
| `personality_match` | How well the companion's traits match the player's interaction style |

Atlas uses these metrics to tune companion behavior between sessions
(see [08-ATLAS_INTEGRATION.md](08-ATLAS_INTEGRATION.md)).

Before building against this document, complete the following research:

- [ ] **Virtual companion research** — Study research on children's relationships with virtual agents. Review Cynthia Breazeal's social robotics work (MIT Media Lab), Sesame Workshop's AI companion studies, and Microsoft's Xiaoice engagement research. Understand parasocial relationship dynamics.
- [ ] **TTS voice personality** — Evaluate Fish Audio voice cloning for creating a consistent companion personality across thousands of lines. Test emotional modulation capabilities. Compare with Qwen3-TTS for secondary voices. Benchmark quality vs. latency.
- [ ] **Dialogue generation** — Research dialogue systems that maintain personality consistency across long interactions. Study persona-based dialogue models (PersonaChat dataset). Evaluate whether LLM-generated dialogue can maintain character over years.
- [ ] **Fourth-wall design (Dora model)** — Study the Dora the Explorer production bible and Sesame Workshop's "pause and wait" technique. Research the developmental psychology behind direct address for 2-5 year olds. Interview early childhood educators on effective techniques.
- [ ] **Companion evolution in games** — Analyze how companion characters evolve in long games: Cortana (Halo series), Ellie (The Last of Us), Elizabeth (BioShock Infinite). What makes a companion feel like a real relationship?
